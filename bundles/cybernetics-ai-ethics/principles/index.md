# Concepts

* [Kantian Deontology & the Categorical Imperative](categorical-imperative-universalizability.md) - Normative ethical framework requiring actions to follow maxims that can be universalized without contradiction and treating rational agents never merely as means, but always as ends in themselves.
* [Goodhart's Law & Campbell's Law under Extremal Optimization](goodharts-law.md) - When an empirical statistical proxy measure becomes the target of high-pressure optimization, the proxy decouples from the true underlying value it was intended to track.
* [Hume's Is–Ought Guillotine (Fact–Value Distinction)](humes-is-ought-guillotine.md) - David Hume's meta-ethical principle that normative "ought" prescriptions cannot be deductively derived solely from descriptive "is" propositions about the physical world.
* [Ashby's Law of Requisite Variety ("Only Variety Can Destroy Variety")](law-of-requisite-variety.md) - Cybernetic theorem proving that for a regulator to maintain stability in a system facing environmental disturbances, the variety of regulator states must be at least as large as the disturbance variety.
* [Negative Feedback & Teleological Purpose (Wiener–Rosenblueth–Bigelow)](negative-feedback-teleology.md) - Foundational 1943/1948 cybernetic principle defining purposeful behavior as circular causal action governed by continuous error-correcting negative feedback relative to a goal state.
* [Second-Order Cybernetics (Cybernetics of Observing Systems)](second-order-cybernetics.md) - Heinz von Foerster and Margaret Mead's extension of cybernetics in which the observer/controller is recognized as an endogenous participant inside the feedback loop rather than an external objective fixture.
* [Consequentialism & Utilitarian Expected-Utility Maximization](utilitarian-expected-value-calculus.md) - Normative framework evaluating actions strictly by the impartial aggregate well-being or expected utility of their consequences across sentient beings.
